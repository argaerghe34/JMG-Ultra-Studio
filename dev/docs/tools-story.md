### Story – interviews into a cut

For documentaries, charity films and anything built from interviews: find the best lines across hours of interviews, sort them by theme and build the cut. Story is the main tool, so it sits first, right under **Home**, where a Story card shows your progress and a button for each step. Story keeps everything with each project, and your original clips are never changed.

Story works in **five steps**, in the order you work: **1 Interviews · 2 Themes · 3 Find · 4 Build · 5 Share**. Each step says what it's for and ends with a **Next** button, and a ✓ marks the steps that are done. You can go back to any step at any time, and 3 Find is optional.

**Story travels with the project.** Everything in Story is saved with the project too, in a **JMG Ultra Studio** folder next to the `.prproj` (*<project> – Story.json*), so opening the project on another computer – your PC and your Mac, from the same drive or a copy of the folder – opens Story where you left it. Reading the transcripts again there keeps every theme, narrative and moment, even though the drive's path is different. If Story was changed on both computers without the copies meeting (say the drive was away), it opens the newer one and asks; the other is kept as a backup in the same folder, so nothing is lost. Home says where Story is saved. To keep Story on this computer only, switch off **Settings › Story › Keep Story next to the project** – worth doing if you share the project folder with people who shouldn't read the transcripts.

AI is optional: every step works without it. With an AI key (Settings), extra switches appear, named after your AI model (e.g. *Let Claude Opus 5.5 choose the lines*). Only the text of transcript lines is sent, never footage.

**1 Interviews – get the transcripts and choose what to work on.**
- Transcribe in Premiere as usual (select the clips › right-click › **Transcribe** – free, on your computer, and it tells speakers apart). Then press **Read Premiere's transcripts**: Ultra Studio saves the project and reads every transcript straight from it. On a real 30-hour project it read 222 transcripts in under 2 seconds. **Import transcript files…** takes SRT, VTT, CSV, JSON or text with timecodes from other services.
- **What to work on** decides what Themes, Find and Moments read: **All interviews**, **Some interviews** (tick them), or **A sequence** – your selects sequence or a rough cut. With a sequence, Story reads which lines are heard in it and works only on those; tick **Only between its In and Out points** to use just the part you've marked (I and O in the Timeline). It reads the sequence again each time, so changes to the cut count. Every step that uses it shows **Working on: …** with a **Change** button.
- **Interviews and speakers** (folded away) is the technical part: **Link to clip** for a transcript that didn't match its clip by file name (Story warns you if one isn't linked), and real names for the speakers, typed once.

**2 Themes – your themes and Story's, in one pass.**
- Type the themes you want under **Your themes**, separated by commas (*Gratitude, first day in Israel, faith*) – or leave it empty – and press **Find themes**. Story finds your themes and up to 10 of its own **in the same pass**: with AI, it reads the text of your lines once, so nothing is paid for twice. A theme of yours that the AI finds nothing for is filled from Story's own search. Without AI (**Without AI**, or no key), your themes are found by their words and the rest are grouped by topic.
- Finding themes again replaces the found ones and looks for yours again; lines you added to a theme by hand stay, and so do the colours.
- **Add one more theme** (folded away) adds a single theme later without reading everything again – Story looks only at lines that could be about it. It's added to **Your themes** too.
- Each theme has a colour swatch and a **⋯** menu to rename, recolour, **Move up** / **Move down**, give it its own **How picky**, **Find more** for it, or delete it. Tick two or more to **Merge ticked**. **Show all** opens a theme, where × takes a line out.

**How picky** decides which lines each theme uses – and with it the sequence you build, bins, Find more and the narrative:
- **Everything useful** – every line that touches the theme, including short and partial ones.
- **Balanced** – good soundbites that match the theme (the default).
- **Only the best** – strong, complete sentences that match closely.

Each choice shows its line count. **Fine-tune** goes in between, and **More options** sets a minimum per interview (so quieter voices aren't dropped), most lines per theme, interviewer questions, short or partial lines, and shortest and longest line. Interviewer questions are left out unless you include them – even when Premiere's transcripts have no speaker names, Story recognises a question put to the person (*“What would you say…?”*, *“Are you…?”*), and a line that starts or ends with one ranks lower. The speaker's own *“Can you imagine?”* isn't treated as a question to them, and a speaker you rename *Interviewer* in Story counts as the interviewer.

**How much the AI reads.** Find themes, Add one more theme, Find more and the narrative cut each have a **How much … reads** slider, from *Best 600* (fewer for the smaller jobs) up to *Everything*. The line under it shows the number of lines, the cost, the requests and roughly how long, before anything runs.

**3 Find – specific lines and moments** (optional). Three buttons share one search box, all within what you chose to work on:
- **Search** – a word, an idea or a quoted phrase (*volunteering*, *"open door"*). It also finds related words and mis-heard spellings. **Strong soundbites only** hides questions, fillers and half sentences. ▶ plays a line in the Source Monitor, from exactly where it starts. Tick lines, then **Add to theme…**, **New theme from these** or **Build sequence**.
- **Find more** – everything on a topic that isn't in a theme yet, each with a reason why. Tick **Not already in my cut** and pick a sequence to leave out lines you've used. Every theme has its own **Find more** too, so you can go back to a topic later.
- **Moments** – **Find moments** looks for **Bloopers** (restarts, retakes, stumbles, *"are we rolling?"*, swearing), **Funny** (laughter), **Off camera** (a second voice, the interviewer reacting) and **Poignant** (emotional words, long pauses mid-thought). Everyday speech doesn't count: an *“and, and”*, a few seconds' thinking, starting sentences the same way on purpose, or an ordinary *“I'm so sorry…”* or *“we had to start again”*. Without AI it works from the words and timing; with AI each moment is checked and described. **How picky** sets how many show and, with AI, how many are checked (the cost shows first). Filter by type. **Build these moments** makes a new sequence grouped by type: with **Colour by type** each type keeps its own colour (bloopers Rose, funny Mango, off camera Teal, poignant Purple) and with **Label each moment** every clip gets a marker saying what it is (*Funny – laughter*). **Make themes of these** makes a theme per type (*Moments · Funny* and so on) in the same colours, to build, sort into bins or share. Or **Mark on string-out** to add range markers coloured by type (bloopers red, funny orange, off camera cyan, poignant purple). **Remove markers** takes them off.

**4 Build – make it in Premiere.**
- **Build a sequence from your themes** lays out each theme in turn, in the order of the Themes list, with the lines How picky keeps and small handles. **Themes to include** switches themes on or off. Under **In the timeline**: **Colour each theme** gives every theme's clips its label colour; **Highlight the strongest lines** gives them their own colour (Yellow unless you change it) and a ★ marker; **Strongest lines first in each theme** puts them first; and **Theme names in the timeline** – **Markers**, **Name layer** (a clip named after the theme on the track above, spanning exactly its clips, using your project's adjustment layer if there is one), **Both** or **Off**.
- **Narrative from a brief**: type something like *a 60-second video about how the community looks after each other* (15 seconds to 10 minutes; 60 s if you don't say) and press **Make the narrative**. Story makes a **Hook**, **Build** and **Payoff** from your themes and other lines on the topic, fitted to the length. With AI it also chooses the order, tightens lines by a few words and says what each one does. Then press **Build this narrative**, or **Paper edit** for a document of it. Under the narrative, **Make it a promo** builds it and then covers its jump cuts with B-roll from a bin you choose (**B-roll from**), adds **Social** or **Subtitles** captions timed to the words, and makes **A vertical 9:16 version too** at full size (1080×1920) – each step a new sequence, ready for Reels, TikTok and Shorts.
- **Sort selects into bins** makes a bin per theme (*Selects – Family*) with its lines as subclips, best first, in the theme's colour. Optionally **Also make a sequence per theme**.
- **Highlight selects in a string-out**: pick your string-out of all the interviews and press **Highlight selects**. Every selected line (from themes, your narratives or sequences Story built – tick which) gets a clip in its theme's colour on a new top track called **Selects**, named after its theme. Optionally **Also add range markers**. **Show what's missed** shows how much of each interview is selected, with a bar you can click and a **Biggest gap** button, without changing the sequence. **Remove highlights** takes them off again.

**5 Share – documents to send.** As **Word**, **Spreadsheet** or **Text**, saved in *Documents › JMG Ultra Studio › Transcripts*.
- **Save the interview log**: a section for each interview – who speaks and for how long, the themes it feeds (with how many lines and where they start), its five strongest lines and its moments, all with the clip's own timecodes. Tick **A short summary of each interview** (with AI) for two or three sentences on who it is and what they talk about; it shows the cost first (none with Gemini), sends only the transcript text, and keeps the summaries, so only new or changed interviews are summarised next time. Tick **Include the full transcripts** for every interview's transcript too, each starting on a new page.
- **Paper edit of your themes**: every theme in order (the ones switched on in Build) with its lines, speakers, clip, source timecodes and length, ★ for the strongest. **Paper edit of the narrative** does the same for your newest narrative, with what each line does.
- The Assistant understands *“write an interview log”*, *“summarise each interview”*, *“a paper edit of the themes”* and *“all the interview transcripts as one Word document”*.

**A transcript of any cut** (also in **5 Share**). Pick a sequence and press **Make transcript**. Story lists who says what in timeline order, keeping only the words you used. It leaves out muted tracks and music beds, and lists any clip still to transcribe.
- **Who is who:** each voice with its talk time, a sample line and ▶. Type names once; the same name in two rows is one person. Unnamed voices become Speaker A, B, C…
- Click a paragraph to jump there. **Export transcript** as **Word**, **Text**, **Subtitles** (.srt) or **Spreadsheet** (.csv), saved in *Documents › JMG Ultra Studio › Transcripts*.
- **Captions** puts Premiere captions on the sequence, timed to each word: **Subtitles** (two lines of up to 42 characters), **Social** (a few words at a time, for Reels, TikTok and Shorts) or **One word**, optionally with **Speaker names** and in **Capitals**. They go on a caption track you can style in Window › Essential Graphics and edit like any captions. **Save .srt only** writes the file without changing the sequence.
