What's new in 1.3.20

  • Punch in on the jump cuts (Silence Cut): a switch on the cut copy – every other piece of a shot that was cut is pushed in a little (110 %, or what you choose), so the cuts read as changes of framing, like FireCut's and AutoCut's zoom cuts. B-roll above is left alone; a new shot starts at 100 %. Works in Premiere and Resolve. Ask: punch in on the jump cuts.

What's new in 1.3.19

Only the reference's transcript text is read by the AI; without AI its length and rhythm are used. Ask: use this timeline as a reference.

What's new in 1.3.18

Lines said in other words can be matched by meaning with AI (only text is sent). Then Build the script, in its order, a group per section. Like Jumper's Script Editor and Premiere's Paper Edit, but across every interview at once and in Resolve too. Ask: build from my script.

What's new in 1.3.12

  • Podcast (Cut faster): cuts between the cameras of a video podcast by who is speaking. Say whose mic is whose and what each camera shows (save it as a preset per show); each mic is heard on its own, on this computer, against its own levels, so bleed doesn't steal the shot. What's new in 1.3.11

What's new in 1.3.10

Install (2 minutes)

Mac: unzip the download, then double-click Install on Mac.command. The first time, macOS may say it's from an unidentified developer: right-click the file › Open › Open. Quit Premiere first (the installer waits for you).

Windows: unzip the download (right-click › Extract All), then double-click Install on Windows.bat. If Windows SmartScreen appears, click More info › Run anyway.

Then open Premiere Pro and choose Window › Extensions › JMG Ultra Studio. Dock it wherever you like.

In DaVinci Resolve Studio: use the Resolve download (JMG-Ultra-Studio-Resolve-<version>.zip) – see In DaVinci Resolve Studio below.

First thing to do: on Home, press Check my setup. It really renders some sound and reads some video from your open project, so you know everything works on that computer. Press Copy report if anything fails and send it over.

Updating: run the new installer. Your settings and analysis results are kept. Removing: run Uninstall on Mac / Windows.

In DaVinci Resolve Studio

The same panel runs in DaVinci Resolve Studio 19.1 or later (20 recommended), on a Mac or a PC. The free DaVinci Resolve can't run plugins.

Install: unzip JMG-Ultra-Studio-Resolve-<version>.zip, quit Resolve, then double-click Install on Mac.command or Install on Windows.bat. Resolve's plugin folder is shared by everyone on the computer, so the Mac may ask for your password and Windows for permission. Then open Resolve and choose Workspace › Workflow Integrations › JMG Ultra Studio – it opens in its own window. Resolve's words: this guide is written for Premiere. In Resolve the panel uses Resolve's words – a sequence is a timeline, the Project panel is the Media Pool, Media Encoder is Resolve's render queue (the Deliver page), export presets are render presets, and adjustment layers are adjustment clips.

What works differently in Resolve:

  • Story can't read the transcripts Resolve makes itself (a plugin can't get at them): Transcribe with Resolve (Story › 1 Interviews) has Resolve turn the selected clips' speech into subtitles and reads those (phrase by phrase, without speaker names), or Story reads transcript files. Resolve may not respond while it transcribes.
  • Captions from Story are saved and put in the Media Pool (Captions bin) for you to drag onto the timeline – Resolve can't place a subtitle file by script.
  • ▶ in Story plays the line in the panel's own player; the clip is selected in the Media Pool.
  • Sound Check can't set clip volume from a plugin: it tells you what to change instead of changing it.
  • Highlights in Story use clip colours and markers, and Render ranges use marked ranges, instead of adjustment layers.
  • Selects can't spot faces in Resolve, so it doesn't sort close-ups by face there; everything else in Selects works the same.
  • Resolve projects live in Resolve's database, not in a file next to your footage.
  • Colour and Camera Switch are paused in both editors for now.

How it behaves everywhere

  • Never touches your original cut. Tools that cut or rebuild a sequence make a copy or a new sequence first. New bins go under JMG Ultra Studio. Colour is the exception by nature: it adds its own Lumetri effect to the clips you choose, and leaves any grade you made alone.
  • Always shows what's happening in the status bar at the top: who's working (Ultra Studio, Premiere, Media Encoder, AI), what it's doing, progress, time left, and a Stop button – one click stops after the current step, three quick clicks stop at once (AI requests too). Click the bar for the full activity log.
  • Says which AI it's using – the top bar shows the model, or No AI. Without AI everything still works; the features that work best with AI say they're running their basic version, and Settings › AI › Without AI explains what's different. Settings also shows usage by API key, and What was sent to AI – the exact text of every request, kept on this computer, with an option to see each one before it's sent.
  • Plain errors. Every problem says what went wrong and the one thing to do next. Technical details sit behind Details.
  • Nothing is uploaded. Analysis runs on your computer, using Premiere itself to read every format it can play. AI is optional and only ever sees text.
  • Remembers analysis. Analysing a clip once is enough; moving sliders afterwards is instant.

Tools

Story – interviews into a cut

For documentaries, charity films and anything built from interviews: find the best lines across hours of interviews, sort them by theme and build the cut. Story is the main tool, so it sits first, right under Home, where a Story card shows your progress and a button for each step. Story keeps everything with each project, and your original clips are never changed.

Story works in five steps, in the order you work: 1 Interviews · 2 Themes · 3 Find · 4 Build · 5 Share. Each step says what it's for and ends with a Next button, and a ✓ marks the steps that are done. You can go back to any step at any time, and 3 Find is optional.

Story travels with the project. Everything in Story is saved with the project too, in a JMG Ultra Studio folder next to the .prproj (<project> – Story.json), so opening the project on another computer – your PC and your Mac, from the same drive or a copy of the folder – opens Story where you left it. Reading the transcripts again there keeps every theme, narrative and moment, even though the drive's path is different. If Story was changed on both computers without the copies meeting (say the drive was away), it opens the newer one and asks; the other is kept as a backup in the same folder, so nothing is lost. Home says where Story is saved. To keep Story on this computer only, switch off Settings › Story › Keep Story next to the project – worth doing if you share the project folder with people who shouldn't read the transcripts.

AI is optional: every step works without it. With an AI key (Settings), extra switches appear, named after your AI model (e.g. Let Claude Opus 5.5 choose the lines). Only the text of transcript lines is sent, never footage.

1 Interviews – get the transcripts and choose what to work on.

  • Transcribe in Premiere as usual (select the clips › right-click › Transcribe – free, on your computer, and it tells speakers apart). Then press Read Premiere's transcripts: Ultra Studio saves the project and reads every transcript straight from it. On a real 30-hour project it read 222 transcripts in under 2 seconds. Import transcript files… takes SRT, VTT, CSV, JSON or text with timecodes from other services.
  • What to work on decides what Themes, Find and Moments read: All interviews, Some interviews (tick them), or A sequence – your selects sequence or a rough cut. With a sequence, Story reads which lines are heard in it and works only on those; tick Only between its In and Out points to use just the part you've marked (I and O in the Timeline). It reads the sequence again each time, so changes to the cut count. Every step that uses it shows Working on: … with a Change button.
  • Interviews and speakers (folded away) is the technical part: Link to clip for a transcript that didn't match its clip by file name (Story warns you if one isn't linked), and real names for the speakers, typed once.

2 Themes – your themes and Story's, in one pass.

  • Type the themes you want under Your themes, separated by commas (Gratitude, first day in Israel, faith) – or leave it empty – and press Find themes. Story finds your themes and up to 10 of its own in the same pass: with AI, it reads the text of your lines once, so nothing is paid for twice. A theme of yours that the AI finds nothing for is filled from Story's own search. Without AI (Without AI, or no key), your themes are found by their words and the rest are grouped by topic.
  • Finding themes again replaces the found ones and looks for yours again; lines you added to a theme by hand stay, and so do the colours.
  • Add one more theme (folded away) adds a single theme later without reading everything again – Story looks only at lines that could be about it. It's added to Your themes too.
  • Each theme has a colour swatch and a ⋯ menu to rename, recolour, Move up / Move down, give it its own How picky, Find more for it, or delete it. Tick two or more to Merge ticked. Show all opens a theme, where × takes a line out.

How picky decides which lines each theme uses – and with it the sequence you build, bins, Find more and the narrative:

  • Everything useful – every line that touches the theme, including short and partial ones.
  • Balanced – good soundbites that match the theme (the default).
  • Only the best – strong, complete sentences that match closely.

Each choice shows its line count. Fine-tune goes in between, and More options sets a minimum per interview (so quieter voices aren't dropped), most lines per theme, interviewer questions, short or partial lines, and shortest and longest line. Interviewer questions are left out unless you include them – even when Premiere's transcripts have no speaker names, Story recognises a question put to the person (“What would you say…?”, “Are you…?”), and a line that starts or ends with one ranks lower. The speaker's own “Can you imagine?” isn't treated as a question to them, and a speaker you rename Interviewer in Story counts as the interviewer.

Read once (Claude). Every Story AI feature starts from the same copy of the transcripts you're working on, which Claude keeps for an hour: the first request of the hour reads them in full, every one after reads them at a twentieth of the price. The line under each AI feature says when Claude has them and what a request costs. (With Gemini, or with Settings › AI sharing switched off:) How much the AI reads. Find themes, Add one more theme, Find more and the narrative cut each have a How much … reads slider, from Best 600 (fewer for the smaller jobs) up to Everything. The line under it shows the number of lines, the cost, the requests and roughly how long, before anything runs.

3 Find – specific lines and moments (optional). Three buttons share one search box, all within what you chose to work on:

  • Search – a word, an idea or a quoted phrase (volunteering, "open door"). It also finds related words and mis-heard spellings. Strong soundbites only hides questions, fillers and half sentences. ▶ plays a line in the Source Monitor, from exactly where it starts. Tick lines, then Add to theme…, New theme from these or Build sequence.
  • Questions – paste the questions you asked (one per line; numbers are fine) and press Find the answers: every full answer to each, from where they start answering to where they move on, under its question, best first, with what they say in a few words. Take out any that isn't an answer, then Build the answers (each question's answers together, in its colour, a marker on each) or Save as Word. Start each answer with the question being asked includes the interviewer's question when it's heard. Without AI it finds the lines that share the question's words.
  • Select all / Unselect all ticks every line shown; ticked lines go Add to theme…, New theme from these or Add to a new sequence… with a name you type.
  • Find more – everything on a topic that isn't in a theme yet, each with a reason why. Tick Not already in my cut and pick a sequence to leave out lines you've used. Every theme has its own Find more too, so you can go back to a topic later.
  • Moments – Find moments looks for Bloopers (restarts, retakes, stumbles, "are we rolling?", swearing), Funny (laughter), Off camera (a second voice, the interviewer reacting) and Poignant (emotional words, long pauses mid-thought). Everyday speech doesn't count: an “and, and”, a few seconds' thinking, starting sentences the same way on purpose, or an ordinary “I'm so sorry…” or “we had to start again”. Without AI it works from the words and timing; with AI each moment is checked and described. How picky sets how many show and, with AI, how many are checked (the cost shows first). Filter by type. Build these moments makes a new sequence grouped by type: with Colour by type each type keeps its own colour (bloopers Rose, funny Mango, off camera Teal, poignant Purple) and with Label each moment every clip gets a marker saying what it is (Funny – laughter). Make themes of these makes a theme per type (Moments · Funny and so on) in the same colours, to build, sort into bins or share. Or Mark on string-out to add range markers coloured by type (bloopers red, funny orange, off camera cyan, poignant purple). Remove markers takes them off.

4 Build – make it in Premiere.

  • Build a sequence from your themes lays out each theme in turn, in the order of the Themes list, with the lines How picky keeps and small handles. Themes to include switches themes on or off (All on / All off). Under In the timeline: Colour each theme gives every theme's clips its label colour; Highlight the strongest lines gives them their own colour (Yellow unless you change it) and a ★ marker; Strongest lines first in each theme puts them first; and Theme names in the timeline – Markers, Name layer (a clip named after the theme on the track above, spanning exactly its clips, using your project's adjustment layer if there is one), Both or Off.
  • Narrative from a brief: type something like a 60-second video about how the community looks after each other (15 seconds to 10 minutes; 60 s if you don't say) and press Make the narrative. Story makes a Hook, Build and Payoff from your themes and other lines on the topic, fitted to the length. To make it from a timeline instead – a selects sequence, a string-out of your picks or a rough cut – set Lines from to A timeline and choose the sequence: Story reads which interview lines are heard in it and makes the story only from those (read again each time, so changes to the timeline count). With AI it also chooses the order, tightens lines by a few words and says what each one does. Then press Build this narrative, or Paper edit for a document of it. Under the narrative, Make it a promo builds it and then covers its jump cuts with B-roll from a bin you choose (B-roll from), adds Social or Subtitles captions timed to the words, and makes A vertical 9:16 version too at full size (1080×1920) – each step a new sequence, ready for Reels, TikTok and Shorts.
  • Like a reference cut: choose a timeline in the project (a past edit you like) or A transcript file of any video (.srt, .vtt, timed .txt), and the narrative follows its shape – each beat's job (Hook, Set-up, Turning point, Ending…) and how long it runs, scaled to your length; with no length in the brief it takes the reference's. With AI its transcript is read as text (never its picture or sound); without AI only its length and rhythm are used. Switch Follow off to ignore it. Ask: use this timeline as a reference.
  • Sort selects into bins makes a bin per theme (Selects – Family) with its lines as subclips, best first, in the theme's colour. Optionally Also make a sequence per theme.
  • Highlight selects in a string-out: pick your string-out of all the interviews and press Highlight selects. Every selected line (from themes, your narratives or sequences Story built – tick which) gets a clip in its theme's colour on a new top track called Selects, named after its theme. Optionally Also add range markers. Show what's missed shows how much of each interview is selected, with a bar you can click and a Biggest gap button, without changing the sequence. Remove highlights takes them off again.

5 Share – documents to send. As Word, Spreadsheet or Text, saved in Documents › JMG Ultra Studio › Transcripts.

  • Save the interview log: a section for each interview – who speaks and for how long, the themes it feeds (with how many lines and where they start), its five strongest lines and its moments, all with the clip's own timecodes. Tick A short summary of each interview (with AI) for two or three sentences on who it is and what they talk about; it shows the cost first (none with Gemini), sends only the transcript text, and keeps the summaries, so only new or changed interviews are summarised next time. Tick Include the full transcripts for every interview's transcript too, each starting on a new page.
  • Paper edit of your themes: every theme in order (the ones switched on in Build) with its lines, speakers, clip, source timecodes and length, ★ for the strongest. Paper edit of the narrative does the same for your newest narrative, with what each line does.
  • The Assistant understands “write an interview log”, “summarise each interview”, “a paper edit of the themes” and “all the interview transcripts as one Word document”.

A transcript of any cut (also in 5 Share). Pick a sequence and press Make transcript. Story lists who says what in timeline order, keeping only the words you used. It leaves out muted tracks and music beds, and lists any clip still to transcribe.

  • Who is who: each voice with its talk time, a sample line and ▶. Type names once; the same name in two rows is one person. Unnamed voices become Speaker A, B, C…
  • Click a paragraph to jump there. Export transcript as Word, Text, Subtitles (.srt) or Spreadsheet (.csv), saved in Documents › JMG Ultra Studio › Transcripts.
  • Captions puts Premiere captions on the sequence, timed to each word: Subtitles (two lines of up to 42 characters), Social (a few words at a time, for Reels, TikTok and Shorts) or One word, optionally with Speaker names and in Capitals. They go on a caption track you can style in Window › Essential Graphics and edit like any captions. Save .srt only writes the file without changing the sequence.

Names and terms (1 Interviews): list how names, places and jargon should be spelt and how the transcription heard them, then Apply to the transcripts – everything downstream uses the right spelling, word timings stay exact, and the original is kept (removing a name puts it back). Suggest from the transcripts finds names spelt more than one way. Keep out of AI sends [Name 1] instead of the name and puts it back in the answer.

Beat map (4 Build): choose what to Look for (emotion, humour, information, conflict, surprise) and Score the interviews: each interview becomes a heat strip of its passages – the brighter, the stronger. Show one criterion or the strongest of them. Click a passage to read it, hear it (▶) and Add to the board; drag the board's beats into the order you want and Build the board.

Clips for social (4 Build): Find clips from Your interviews or A timeline (only what's heard in it), at a Length (15–30, 30–60 or 60–90 s), with what they're For if you like (funny moments for TikTok). Each clip shows its hook, its scores (hook, stands alone, payoff, length), why it works and its words, with ▶ to hear it. Tick the ones you want, choose A vertical version too (9:16, 4:5 or 1:1) and Captions, then Build the ticked clips – one sequence each, with the hook as a marker. A clip may join two moments; each part ends on a whole sentence.

From a script (4 Build): paste a script or paper edit – one soundbite per line, Name: in front if you know who says it, # headings for sections, [notes] in brackets. Find the script lines finds each line in the interviews you're working on: word for word where it's said, cut to just those words (the well, you know before it left off), even when it runs over two lines; otherwise the close or loose match, with the next-best takes in a menu – or Leave this line out. With AI on, lines said in other words are matched by meaning: the AI chooses among the closest lines, reading only text. Clips are never stitched from the end of one line and the start of the next. Build the script lays it in the script's order, a group per section, with a marker on any match that isn't word for word. Ask: build from my script.

Length (4 Build): type 3:00 (or 90, 2 min) and the cut is fitted to it ±10% – the weakest lines go first, from the longest themes; pinned lines and each theme's best line stay; if it's still short it says so.

Soundbites (4 Build): every build finishes sentences (running on into the same speaker's next lines, never across to someone else), leaves out the interviewer, lengthens bites shorter than the Shortest soundbite, can start and end on the first and last word (Tight edges), and marks each clip with why it's there.

Narrative extras. Under the brief, Client notes and context takes the client's emails, briefs or documents (paste them, or Load a text file…): the AI follows them when it shapes the story. Alternative selects lists the strong lines that didn't make it – with the AI, each with why (and which beat it could replace) – and Add them at the end of the sequence puts 5, 12, 25, 50, 100 or all of them after the payoff, each with a marker saying why. Keep clips in the order they were filmed (on every build) lays each group's clips interview by interview, in time order. Highlight selects in a string-out can also add range markers on the Timeline, the Clips or Both.

Selects & clips

Selects – the usable moments in any footage

It plays every clip on your computer (nothing is uploaded) and grades its moments BEST (green), GOOD (yellow) and OK (blue). By default it works on any footage: everything you could cut to or put over something else is kept, and only what's technically unusable is left out – blurry or out of focus, far too dark or blown out, too shaky, a camera bump or a whip pan. It names the shot size and every select's camera move – Pan left/right, Tilt up/down, Push-in, Pull-out, Static, Handheld or a named reveal – with how smooth it is (very smooth, smooth, a bit uneven, uneven). It flags reveals (pull-backs, crane moves, lights coming up – in purple), shots that crop well to 9:16, and slow-mo candidates (clips shot at 50 fps or more).

  1. In Works on, choose Selected clips, A bin or A sequence. On a sequence it grades the camera clips on V1 (or All video tracks), skipping graphics, adjustment layers, nested sequences and stills. Each select still points at its source clip.
  2. What counts as usable starts on Any footage – usable B-roll, which suits every kind of shot. Only if you want a style to count too, open Kind of footage (optional) and choose Concert & live music, Interviews & talking heads, Weddings & events, Travel & B-roll, Sports & action, Documentary, Drone & aerial, Phone footage, Crowd & audience or Close-ups – for example Interviews want a steady face, and Sports wants movement.
  3. Camera movement decides how much smooth movement counts: Doesn't matter (technical quality only), Smooth moves count (the default: a smooth pan, tilt, push-in or pull-out ranks higher) or Moves first (smooth moves first, static shots a little lower). Wobbly or jerky moves never get a bonus, and this changes the order, never what's kept. Show › Smooth camera moves lists just those; Sort › Smoothest moves first puts them on top; markers name the move too.
  4. Choose How picky – the same three choices as Story: Everything useful, Balanced or Only the best, each showing how many selects it gives. Fine-tune has a slider for each check and grade, plus Drop duplicate takes.
  5. Press Find selects. It runs at about 16× real time on 1080p and remembers each clip, so changing the footage type, How picky or a slider re-grades instantly.
  6. Look through the list. Sort it (best first, shooting order, shot size…) and Show one kind (a grade, reveals, 9:16, close-ups…, or what was Left out). Each select says why it scored as it did. Click one to see it in the Source Monitor (or in the sequence), Keep a moment it left out, or × to leave one out.
  7. Make it – tick any mix:
    • A selects sequence, colour-coded by grade with a marker on every clip, in the Order you choose. Or One sequence per grade.
    • Sort into bins by type: a bin per category, best first, each in its own colour – by grade, by shot size, by camera move, and Special (hero shots, reveals, 9:16, slow-mo, faces, action). Optionally Also a sequence per category.
    • A bin of subclips by grade, named like 001_BEST_closeup_87.
    • Markers on the source clips with the score and reasons, markers on the sequence you graded, and Colour labels on the source clips.
    • Stills of the best frames: a full-size PNG of the sharpest frame in each select (sharp, steady, faces if there are any; up to 50, best first) in Documents › JMG Ultra Studio › Stills – for thumbnails, posts and your photo edit.
    • Mark the best: your top selects (The best (★) in Fine-tune: top 3, 5, 10 or 10%) get their own colour (magenta), a ★, and go first.
    • Include: All, BEST + GOOD or BEST only.

Nothing changes in your project until you press Make it; new items go in JMG Ultra Studio › Selects. Remove Selects markers takes the markers off again.

Formats the panel can't read. The panel reads H.264 MP4 files directly, and uses a clip's proxy if it has one. For 10-bit 4:2:2 (Lumix and others), iPhone HEVC, ProRes or RAW, leave Make small analysis copies of clips the panel can't read on: Media Encoder makes a 1280×720 copy with Premiere's own High Quality 720 HD preset. The copies stay in Ultra Studio's own folder, and times always match the original. They aren't added to your project unless you tick Also attach them as proxies (16:9 clips only).

Subclip Maker – browse only the parts you chose

Make subclips of:

  • Clips in a bin: every clip in it becomes a subclip of only the part between the In and Out you set on it (in Resolve: Mark In and Mark Out). Clips without In/Out are kept whole.
  • Clips on the timeline: every clip in the open sequence becomes a subclip of the part used in the edit, in timeline order. Nested sequences, titles and adjustment layers are left out, and a clip's sound isn't made twice.

The subclips have restricted In/Out, so long clips show just that part in thumbnails and when you scrub. Your originals aren't changed.

  • Put them in a new bin: next to the original bin, "<bin> – Subclips" (or "<sequence> – Subclips" for the timeline), or type another name. Or choose a bin you choose from the list.
  • Add handles (off by default) adds a few seconds before and after each subclip (you set how many), never past the ends of the clip.
  • They're numbered in filming order (timeline order for the timeline) with the original file name: 01 – C0012.MP4.
  • Only clips directly in the bin are used, not clips in bins inside it.
  • If subclips with the same names are already there, you're asked first: Replace moves the earlier ones into "<bin> – Subclips – earlier" (add-ons can't delete clips – delete that bin yourself), Add alongside names the new ones (2), (3)…

Take Sets – the best take of each retake

For pieces to camera, interviews with do-overs and podcast reads. Pick the sequence and press Find the takes: every set of attempts at the same line is grouped – word for word, reworded, cut short or picked up again minutes later (with AI; without it, retakes that share most of their words). Paste the Script if there is one: sets are named after its lines and judged against it.

  • Each set lists its takes, marked complete or incomplete, with a note on how they differ and ▶ to hear them. The last complete take is chosen; pick another, or Keep every take when they aren't really retakes.
  • What will be cut lists every unchosen take (with the pause after it) – untick any to keep it.
  • Make a copy with the chosen takes cuts the others out of a copy of the sequence, on every track together so nothing goes out of sync, and puts a marker on each kept take. Words from two takes are never stitched together. The original isn't changed.
  • Takes are found from Story's transcripts of the clips in the sequence (only their text goes to the AI). Ask: keep the best takes.

MC Selects – the cameras you keep from a multicam edit

Open a cut multicam edit (the multicam clips on V1) and press Read the cameras. Every camera is listed by its file name with a still of it (the file's own preview, as Finder or Explorer shows it – nothing is rendered; switch off Show a still of each camera to hide them), the camera model when the file says (📷 Sony ILCE-7SM3, Panasonic DC-S1M2, GoPro HERO11, Apple iPhone 15 Pro), how many cuts it has and how long it's on screen. Under the list, the total running time of the cameras you keep – what the new sequence will run. Keep the ones you want and press Make the MC Selects sequence: a new sequence of just their shots, back to back in the order you edited them, each cut its own clip with no handles. Give a camera a label colour to see it at a glance, and switch on Include the camera’s scratch audio for the camera's own sound. Your choices are remembered.

Works with Multi-Camera Source Sequences and with nested sequences switched to multicam. Ultra Studio saves the project first, because which camera each cut shows is only written in the saved project. Premiere only: Resolve doesn't let add-ons see which camera a multicam cut shows.

Find the good bits

Word cloud – the words said most

The words and phrases said most in your interviews, drawn as a cloud: the bigger the word, the more it's said. It reads Story's transcripts – the interviews (or the sequence) chosen in Story › 1 Interviews › What to work on – and counts on this computer; nothing is sent anywhere.

  • Who: everyone but the interviewer (the default), everyone, or one person – to hear what each of them keeps coming back to.
  • Word forms together counts community and communities as one word; Phrases adds two- and three-word phrases said in at least two lines (local community, peace of mind). Small words and fillers (the, um, you know) are left out. Hide this word takes one out of the cloud; Show hidden words again brings them back.
  • Click a word to list every time it's said: the words around it, who said it, the interview and the time. ▶ plays it (in Premiere's Source Monitor; in Resolve, in the panel). Shift-click to pick several words. Find looks up any word or phrase, even one not in the cloud.
  • Build a sequence of the mentions: Just the words (a supercut – each mention with a little air either side), the Whole line, or the Full sentence. Untick any you don't want. With several words picked, each gets its own group and colour. Each clip has a marker with the word and its line.
  • List shows the words ranked with their counts; How many words sets the size. Copy the counts or Save as spreadsheet for a document or a report.
  • Order: As said, or Mix the voices – taking turns between the people saying it, so one voice doesn't run on.
  • Add to Story as a theme puts every line it's said in into a theme of yours.
  • Ask: word cloud, or build every time they say "community".

Song Splitter – concerts and events

Point it at the sound-desk recording (a clip or WAV) or your multicam sequence and press Find the songs. It finds each song, the applause, talk and the interval, and handles songs that run straight into each other.

  • How many songs: if it finds too many (a solo or a quiet bridge counted as a new song) or too few, move Fewer songs – more songs. Fewer: only applause or long talk between songs starts a new one, and short songs join their neighbours. More: a change of key or tempo counts too. Or type the number of songs played if you know the setlist: it keeps the strongest breaks that give that many. Both re-sort what it already heard, straight away, without listening again. Titles you've typed stay with their songs.
  • The list is editable: rename songs (it can't recognise titles, so type them), join or split parts at a time, change a part's type, and click to jump there.
  • Add to Premiere can add any combination of: a marker for each song (and for applause and talk too), a subclip for each song, and a sequence for each song. On a sequence it makes trimmed copies, one per song.
  • Adjustment layers above the edit (on a sequence): a new top track called Songs with an adjustment layer for each song, numbered 1, 2, 3… (with its title once you've renamed it), plus the talk and applause, colour-coded – songs green, talk blue, applause orange. Name the songs in the Timeline, then Deliver › Render ranges renders each one: choose the Songs track and the songs' colour. Running it again replaces the layers; Remove song layers takes them away.
  • Copy or Save the setlist with timecodes (text and spreadsheet).
  • Remove my markers takes away only the markers it added.

Cut faster

Podcast – cut between the cameras by who is speaking

For a synced video podcast: the cameras stacked on video tracks, each person's mic on its own audio track. Press Read the tracks, name each mic after its person (leave a track empty if it isn't a mic) and say what each camera shows – one person, or Wide / everyone. Save as a preset to reuse the setup on every episode.

  • Listen to the mics: each mic is rendered on its own (the others muted for a moment, then put back) and measured on this computer against its own quiet and talking levels – so a voice that bleeds into another mic doesn't steal the shot, and two people talking at once count as crosstalk. Nothing leaves the computer.
  • How it cuts: Long-form, Standard or Promo, or set the Shortest shot (a quick reply doesn't flash a cut) and Longest shot (then a reaction shot of someone listening, or the wide) yourself. Cut after the voice starts a few frames late, as an editor would; ignore replies like yeah and mm-hm; wide when people talk over each other, on long silences, and at the start and the end; cutaways land on sentence ends when Story has transcripts. Shuffle the variety picks differently (and repeatably) when there's more than one choice.
  • Changing a rule redraws the edit at once: a coloured strip of the shots, how much each camera is used, and every switch in a list – untick one to stay on the shot before, ▶ to see it.
  • Make the switched copy: a copy of the sequence with every camera still stacked, cut at each switch and only the active one switched on. To change a shot later, switch a piece on (or the one above it off). The original isn't changed.
  • In DaVinci Resolve the same, except that Resolve can't cut clips by script, so each camera track is laid again in pieces from the same source times. Resolve Studio's own SmartSwitch is another option there.
  • The Podcast episode recipe (Ask › Recipes) switches the cameras, cuts the fillers and false starts from the switched copy, and writes the episode text.

Silence Cut – tighten interviews

Pick a sequence (the active one by default) and press Find the pauses. The pauses show on a strip under the waveform. Then choose a preset:

  • Natural – only clear pauses.
  • Tight – a good default for interviews.
  • Very tight – social-media pace.
  • Podcast – long gaps only.

Or fine-tune silence level (Auto level sets it from the room noise), shortest pause to cut, and how much to keep around speech. The numbers update as you move the sliders.

Make a cut copy duplicates the sequence and ripple-cuts every pause across all tracks, so picture and sound stay in sync. The original is never touched. Or use Add markers instead to decide by hand. Locked tracks are flagged first, because cutting around them would knock them out of sync.

Fillers and false starts – below the pauses: Find fillers and false starts reads the words in Story's transcripts of the clips in the sequence and lists every um, uh, er, doubled small word (the the) and restarted sentence, each with the words around it. Untick any you want to keep (filter by kind, Tick all / Untick all), press ▶ to hear one, then Make a cut copy – with Cut the pauses too when you've found them, both go in one copy. Soft fillers (, you know,) are off unless you switch them on. Cuts are exact with Premiere's word timings; in Resolve (times per phrase) they're estimated and left unticked.

  • Punch in on the jump cuts (with either cut button): in the cut copy, every other piece of a shot that was cut is pushed in a little – Push in to 104–130 %, 110 % by default – so each cut reads as a change of framing, the way an editor hides a jump cut. Only pieces of the same shot that meet at a cut change; a new shot starts at 100 %, and B-roll on the tracks above is left alone. It uses the clip's own Scale (Zoom in Resolve), so it's easy to change by hand. Ask: punch in on the jump cuts (on the open sequence – run it on a copy).

Beat Cut – cut to the music

Premiere has no beat detection; this adds it. Select a music clip and press Find the beat. It shows the tempo; if it's double or half what you hear, use Double speed / Half speed.

  • Add markers: every beat, every 2 beats, every bar or every 2 bars. Use them with Premiere's Automate to Sequence › At unnumbered markers.
  • Make the beat cut: pick a bin of clips (your Selects work well), a length (15 s, 30 s, 60 s or the whole song), the cut rhythm, the order (bin order or shuffled), and optionally faster cuts in louder parts. You get a new sequence with the music on A1 and a shot on every cut. If Selects has analysed the clips, each shot starts at its best moment.

B-roll – cover jump cuts

When you cut interviews down, the picture jumps wherever two pieces of the same interview meet. B-roll lays a cutaway over each join while the sound carries on, using the best moments from your B-roll – on a copy of the sequence, so your cut is never changed.

  1. Open the sequence and check Talking heads on (usually V1). Cover chooses where cutaways go: Jump cuts (joins inside the same interview), Cuts between people too (also cuts back to someone you've already seen – a person is never covered the first time they appear) or Long stretches (a cutaway every 5–30 s in long talking heads, set with A cutaway every).
  2. Set the Cutaway length and how long it Starts before the cut. The list shows every spot; click one to jump there. Spots that already have B-roll over them are left alone.
  3. Under B-roll clips, pick a bin (or select clips in the Project panel). With Pick the best moments with Selects on, the B-roll is analysed like Selects does (Kind of footage starts on Any footage: only technically unusable moments are left out) and the sharpest, steadiest moments are used first, each only once and never the same clip twice in a row.
  4. Press Make a B-roll copy. You get “your sequence – B-roll” with the cutaways on a new track called B-roll, picture only (switch on Keep the B-roll's own sound if you want it). The track is chosen so no interview or music is ever overwritten.

Works well after Story's narrative cut. The Assistant understands “cover the jump cuts with B-roll”.

Camera Switch – cut between interview angles

For interviews filmed on two or more cameras: it cuts between the angles at the ends of sentences, on a copy, without touching the sound.

  1. Put the angles on top of each other, synced, on V1, V2 (and V3…). Premiere can sync them: select the clips › right-click › Synchronize. Open that sequence – Camera Switch lists the angles under Cameras; switch off any you don't want, and they're left out of the copy.
  2. Choose Alternate angles (one person: it goes round the cameras, starting on the one you pick in Start on) or Follow who's speaking (it shows each person's camera – say Whose camera is whose – keeps quick replies on the shot before, and cuts long answers to the wide and back).
  3. Set the Shortest shot and Longest shot. The coloured strip shows the plan; click a shot to jump there.
  4. Press Make a camera cut copy. You get “your sequence – camera cut”: the upper camera tracks are cut so the chosen angle shows, and the angle on the lowest track runs whole underneath, so you can fine-tune by trimming.

Only one camera? Camera Switch punches in instead: every other shot is zoomed (Punch-in, 105–140%, 115% to start), so a single-camera interview cuts like two angles.

With Premiere's transcripts read into Story, the cuts fall between sentences and it knows who is speaking; without them it cuts in the pauses (and alternates). The Assistant understands “cut between the cameras”.

Finish & deliver

Colour – looks, LUTs, grading and matching cameras

Everything Colour adds is an ordinary Lumetri Color effect, so you can open it in Effect Controls and carry on by hand. Ultra Studio adds its own Lumetri to a clip the first time and only ever changes that one – a grade you made yourself is never touched. Apply to chooses the clips: Selected clips in the timeline, the Clip at the playhead, or All clips on V1.

Previews appear by themselves. With Colour open, park the playhead on a shot: a moment later Ultra Studio takes that frame from your sequence and shows the looks, LUTs or grade on it – no button, and no extra sequence. The large viewer shows the one you've chosen with Before / After; ← → (or the arrow buttons) step through them, and the strip of shots under it jumps around the sequence.

Looks. Premiere's own looks, previewed on your shot. The first time you open a family, press Get … looks ready once: Premiere renders each look on a test image (it briefly shows a hidden preview sequence) and Ultra Studio keeps it as a colour table, so from then on every preview is instant on any shot. The colour in the preview is exact (film looks match Premiere's own render pixel for pixel); a look that also blurs, sharpens or vignettes – SpeedLooks soften a little – does that part only when applied. Choose a family – Film (Fuji and Kodak film stocks, Cinespace), SpeedLooks (with a Camera choice; Universal suits most footage), Black & white or Technical – or type in Filter looks. Pick one, set the Strength and press Apply this look. Each look goes on as its own Lumetri effect, so you can switch it off or delete it.

LUTs. Your own LUT files (.cube). Ultra Studio lists the LUTs in Adobe's LUT folders, and Add a LUT file… adds any other. Each one is previewed on your shot straight from the file. Apply this LUT puts it into Ultra Studio's Lumetri (Creative › Look) at the Strength you choose. Lumetri can only use LUTs from its Look menu: if Premiere doesn't know a LUT yet, Ultra Studio offers to copy it to the right folder, and it's ready after you restart Premiere. The first LUT you apply in a session takes about 20 seconds while Ultra Studio finds it in the menu.

Grade. A quick grade with a live preview made by Ultra Studio (instant; Exact preview asks Premiere to render it). Start from Natural, Warm, Cool, Punchy, Soft, Moody or Black & white, then adjust Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Warmth, Tint, Saturation, Vibrance, Faded film and Vignette. Apply grade grades the clips; grading again changes the same Lumetri rather than stacking another. Reset Ultra Studio's grade on these clips puts it back to neutral.

Match. Makes other cameras look like your main one.

  1. Park the playhead on the shot whose colour you like (camera A).
  2. Select the clips from the other camera(s) in the timeline.
  3. Press Match selected clips to the shot at the playhead.

Ultra Studio measures the reference at the playhead and each clip on its own (angles stacked above it are switched off while it looks), then adjusts exposure, contrast, warmth, tint and saturation step by step, checking Premiere's picture each time, until they match. The list shows the difference before and after for each clip (under 3 is hard to see). It works best on shots from the same event and time of day. If one camera shot log, set it up in Log first.

Log. Footage filmed in a log profile (Lumix V-Log, Sony S-Log, DJI D-Log, Canon Log and others) looks flat and grey. Select the clips and press Check these clips: Ultra Studio shows how Premiere is reading each one (Premiere often recognises log files by itself – your Lumix V-Log clips show as converted by Premiere), which look flat, and which camera they probably came from (Lumix files named like P1003525.MOV suggest Panasonic V-Log/V-Gamut). Choose the Camera profile and press Apply camera profile; you see the shot before and after. Premiere then converts the clip to normal colour itself – usually better than a conversion LUT. This is the same as right-click › Modify › Color in Premiere, so it changes the clip everywhere it's used in the project; choose The clip's own colour (undo) to put it back. Needs Premiere Pro 2025 or later.

The Assistant understands requests like “match the cameras”, “black and white”, “make it warmer” and “these clips are V-Log”.

Safe Zones – social guides and letterboxing

Two modes, chosen at the top:

Social guides show the 9:16, 4:5 and 1:1 crops and where TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and Facebook put their buttons, captions and top bar, so titles and subtitles stay clear.

  • Tick the crops and apps and check the preview (Use current frame shows it over your shot).
  • Look: Colours & labels (red app areas, a green safe box, names) or Plain grey (just grey areas and thin lines, no colours or text).
  • Put guide on the sequence adds it on a top track called Guides for the whole length. Nothing underneath moves; adding another replaces the old one.
  • Before exporting, press Hide for export. Premiere can't show a clip but leave it out of an export, so the guide track gets switched off. Final Check and Deliver both catch it if you forget.

Letterbox adds bars to any picture shape (2.39:1, 2:1, 1.85:1, 4:3, 1:1, 4:5, 9:16) without any social guides:

  • Black bars are part of your film: they go on their own top track called Letterbox and they export. Change the shape any time and the bars are replaced.
  • Grey guide is a see-through framing preview that is hidden for export like the other guides.

Save PNG pack writes the guides as images (1080p, 4K, vertical) for any other editor. Guide images are kept in a “JMG Ultra Studio Guides” folder next to your project, so they move with it. App layouts are approximate and change from time to time.

Sound Check – before you upload

Pick the target – YouTube & social (−14 LUFS), Podcast (−16) or Broadcast UK/EBU (−23) – and press Check the sound on a sequence or selected clips. It measures loudness the way YouTube, Spotify and broadcasters do. Then it lists anything to fix, each with a plain instruction (e.g. “Raise the mix by about 3.5 dB”) and a jump-to button:

  • clipping and peaks too hot
  • mains hum
  • wind and rumble
  • silent gaps

Checking never changes your sequence. When the loudness is off, Make a copy at −14 LUFS (or the target you chose) makes a copy with every clip's level changed by the right amount – held back if the peaks would go over the limit – and measures the copy again, so you see it land on target.

Duck the music under speech makes a copy of the active sequence with the music lowered while people speak (Duck by, 6–24 dB), fading down just before each sentence and back up after. It suggests the music tracks (audio-only files such as WAV or MP3, never a track with transcribed speech – check them in Music on, especially if you record interview sound separately) and the speech from the transcripts, or from the sound when there are none.

Final Check – before you export

Press Check the cut on the active sequence. It never changes anything; it lists:

  • Fix before export: black gaps, flash frames (clips of 1–2 frames), offline media, a Safe Zones guide still showing.
  • Worth a look: clips switched off, the same shot used twice, holes in the sound, open notes (red markers or markers starting “TODO”/“FIX”).
  • For info: speed changes, locked tracks, and (if you set it) shots longer than a set length.

Click any line to jump the playhead there. Add markers for these marks them on the timeline; Remove check markers takes them off again.

Check the project lists clips that aren't used in any sequence, offline media (relink them with right-click › Link Media) and anything imported twice. Move them into a bin “Unused” tidies the unused clips away – nothing is deleted – and Put them back returns each one to where it was.

Compare two versions lists what changed between an older and a newer cut: shots added, removed, trimmed and moved, plus the change in length. It's handy after a round of client notes. It picks the previous version automatically when they're named like “Promo v2” / “Promo v3”.

Deliver – every export in one go

Export pack – tick what you need and press Export:

  • Master in the sequence's own shape (H.264, high quality).
  • Vertical 9:16, Square 1:1, Portrait 4:5, made with Premiere's Auto Reframe as new sequences in JMG Ultra Studio › Deliver (so you can adjust the framing and re-export), at full platform size: 1080×1920, 1080×1080, 1080×1350 (or 2160 wide from a 4K edit).
  • Sound only (WAV 48 kHz) and a thumbnail (the frame under the playhead).

Exports go through Media Encoder, and the status bar shows its real progress. Files land in Documents › JMG Ultra Studio › Exports › <sequence and date>, or a folder you choose. Earlier exports are never overwritten. Safe Zones guides are hidden automatically first.

Render ranges – several renders from one sequence, each a part you mark yourself:

  1. Ranges are: Adjustment layers (as before), Text layers, or Both. A text layer shows its name in the Timeline; its length is the render and the words it shows become the file name. With Text layers, Add a render range adds a text layer from Premiere's Basic Title template. Keep the text out of the renders (on by default) switches the track off while the renders go to Media Encoder and on again afterwards, so the text isn't in the files – nor are effects on that track's adjustment layers. In Resolve, Text and Text+ titles count when they're on a video track named like the ranges (e.g. Renders); rename and move them in the Timeline.
  2. Choose the Track whose adjustment layers are the ranges – the list shows every video track (V3 · Renders · 4 layers), and New track on top for render ranges adds an empty one above everything: Renders (made the first time you add a range), or any track that holds adjustment layers – for example Songs, where Song Splitter puts its songs.
  3. Type a name and press Add a render range. An adjustment layer goes on that track, between your In and Out points (or for 10 seconds from the playhead), in the colour you pick next to the button. You can also put adjustment layers there yourself, from the Project panel.
  4. Each range is listed with its colour, times and length. Click one to go there. Rename gives it (and its file) a name: Teaser 30s → Teaser 30s.mp4. Times… sets its start and end as the Timeline shows them, or to your In and Out points (type In/Out). Colour changes its colour, and Delete takes it off the timeline. You can still trim and rename layers in the Timeline: the list follows the Timeline by itself within a few seconds (Refresh does it at once).
  5. Colour: render every range, or only the ranges of one colour – for example only the songs, not the talk and applause.
  6. Pick the Export settings: the built-in H.264 presets, any preset you've saved in Media Encoder or in Premiere's Export screen, or Choose a preset file…. Add to every file name puts an ending on each file, such as _1 or _v2 (Song 1_v2.mp4); the export pack uses it too.
  7. Press Send the renders to Media Encoder. Every range is queued in timeline order and rendered one after another, with progress in the status bar. Turn off Start rendering straight away to leave them waiting in Media Encoder's queue instead.

Switch a layer off (Enable, in its right-click menu) to skip it. An empty adjustment layer doesn't change the picture, but any effect you add to one shows in that render. Your own In and Out points are left as they were. If the project has no adjustment layer, an invisible clip is used instead, and it renders the same way.

Colours: Premiere doesn't tell add-ons the colour of a clip in the Timeline, so Ultra Studio knows the colours it gave – from Song Splitter, Add a render range and Colour here. Change a range's colour here rather than in the Timeline, or it won't be picked up by the colour filter.

Chapters – builds a YouTube chapter list from your named markers (0:00 Intro, 1:23 Family…). It starts at 0:00 and warns about YouTube's rules (at least 3 chapters, each 10 seconds or longer). Copy it into the video description.

Episode text – titles, a description, show notes with times, YouTube chapters, pull quotes, social posts and keywords, written from what is said in the open sequence (Story's transcripts of the clips in it; only that text goes to the AI). Add Notes for the writer – the show's name, tone, links – then Write the episode text. Each part has Copy; Save as a text file keeps them all; Add the chapters as markers puts them on the sequence. Quotes the AI returns are checked against the transcript, and chapters follow YouTube's rules (first at 0:00, each at least 10 seconds). Without AI it gives chapters from your named markers and the strongest lines as quotes.

Ask

Assistant – just ask

Type what you want: “cut the silences”, “markers on every beat”, “find lines about family”, “a 60-second video about volunteering”, “cover the jump cuts with B-roll”, “match the cameras”, “cut between the cameras”, “add captions”, “these clips are V-Log”, “check the cut”, “safe zones for Reels”, “export the master and a vertical version”. It shows a plan first – each step in plain words, with the ones that change your project marked – and only acts when you press Run.

Common requests work with no AI key. With a key (Settings), it can handle anything phrased freely and chain several steps. It can only ever run Ultra Studio's own tools.

My interviews (needs AI): switch the Assistant to My interviews and ask anything about what your interviews say – every story about Grandma, in the order it happened, where do the interviewees disagree?, a 45-second opening about why they came, who talks about their parents, and what do they say?. In Anything mode such questions go there by themselves. The answer is a few sentences and the clips, in groups – each clip a whole stretch of speech, with a note on why it's there and its length; the total shows against the length you asked for. Nothing changes in Premiere until you choose: play, move, take out, rename or recolour, then New sequence (each group in turn, in its colour, with a marker on every clip, group names as markers or a name layer, optionally in the order they were filmed), Mark a sequence (range markers on a string-out or your cut), Themes (one per group) or Word or text. Type a change under the answer – shorter, only Ruth, split it before and after the move – and it answers again in full. It reads what you chose to work on in Story, and the answers are kept with the project (Earlier on the welcome).

Edit (needs AI): switch the Assistant to Edit and say what to change – a cross dissolve on every cut on V1, punch in to 110% on each interview clip, dip to black where the speaker changes, lower thirds with the speaker's name the first time each person talks, audio fades of 12 frames on every music clip, colour every clip longer than 20 seconds orange, put all the drone clips in a Drone bin.

  • Where it acts: This sequence, its Selected clips, the Transcript lines heard in it (for markers, text, cuts or the clips under them), A bin, or the Whole project.
  • It reads that as text – clip names, tracks, times, labels, effects and, if your interviews are transcribed in Story, who speaks and what they say (never footage) – and shows a plan: each edit with how many clips it touches (Which lists them; click one to go there). Untick any you don't want.
  • The edits: transitions (and audio fades), effects, speed, scale/position/rotation, slow zooms (punch-in, Ken Burns), opacity, volume, colour labels, switching clips on or off, deleting (closing the gaps or not), renaming, markers, text (titles or lower thirds), cuts across all tracks – and in a bin or the project: labels, names, moving to a bin, markers and a new sequence.
  • Run: sequence edits go on a copy, “<sequence> – Ask”, in JMG Ultra Studio › Ask (switch off Work on a copy to change the sequence itself). Project edits change your clips, and Undo puts labels, names and bins back.
  • Then it checks its own work: it reads the copy back, compares it with what you asked and offers anything still missing as a new plan, on the same copy. Type a change at any time – only on V2, make them 20 frames – and it plans again.
  • In DaVinci Resolve: framing, opacity, colour labels, on/off, deleting and markers. Resolve doesn't let add-ons add transitions, effects, speed changes, keyframes or text, and doesn't say which timeline clips are selected (the clip under the playhead stands in).

Recipes: a chain of steps saved under a name – Get interviews ready, Check and deliver, or your own. After a plan runs, press Save as a recipe; after an Edit, save the request. Run one from the welcome's Recipes chips, All recipes…, or by typing run my … recipe. Steps run one after another on the open project; an edit step shows its plan for you to check before it runs.
