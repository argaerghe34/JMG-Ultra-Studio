### Assistant – just ask

One chat box for everything. Type what you want in your own words: *“cut the silences”*, *“a cross dissolve on every cut on V1”*, *“put all the drone clips in a Drone bin”*, *“every story about Grandma, in the order it happened”*, *“lower thirds with the speaker’s name the first time each person talks”*, *“tighten this cut, then check it”*, *“how do I export just the audio?”*. There are no modes to choose – it works out whether you want a tool run, a sequence or the project changed, a question about your interviews answered, or several of those one after another.

**What you see while it works** (needs AI – a Claude or Gemini key, or a local model, in Settings):
- **What I understand** – the request in its own words, with anything it is assuming (which sequence, which clips).
- **Plan** – the steps it intends.
- Each step as it happens, with what came back (*Read “Main cut” → 3:05, 42 clips*).
- **A question first** – when it isn’t sure, or there’s a choice only you can make: answers to click (or a picker of your own clips, bins, sequences or tracks), or type your own.
- **May I?** – a card before a step that changes something: what it will do and to how many clips (**Which** lists them; click one to go there; untick the ones you don’t want). **Proceed**, **Always proceed** (for steps that only make new things, in this chat) or **Skip**. Typing instead tells it what you’d rather have.
- At the end: what it did, **Done**, **Not done** (with how to do it by hand), **Worth checking**, the sequences it made, follow-ups to click – and **How I got there**: what it understood, the plan, every step, which AI and what it cost.

**What it can do:** read the project, any sequence, a bin or your selection (as text – names, times, tracks, labels, effects, markers and, when transcribed, who speaks and what they say); search and question your interviews (the answer comes as a card of clips you can reorder, build, mark or save – see below); every edit (transitions and audio fades, effects, speed, framing, slow zooms, opacity, volume, labels, on/off, deleting, renaming, markers, text, cuts, moving to bins, new sequences, a clip added to many sequences); every Ultra Studio tool (silences, takes, beats, captions, cameras, safe zones, check the cut, deliver…); building sequences from interview lines – a whole cut from a goal (*“a 2-minute film for the donors about the bakery, ending on hope”*), which is what the Agent tab used to do; keeping versions. It doesn’t watch video or hear sound; what’s in the picture it knows from written descriptions of your clips and, when needed, a few small stills (see *What’s in the footage* below).

**How often it asks** (the first menu under the chats): **Ask before changes** (every step that changes something), **Only for my own work** (copies, new sequences and markers just happen; it asks before changing your own sequences and clips, exporting or downloading), or **Don’t ask**. Reading is never asked about. Sequence edits go on a copy, *“<sequence> – Ask”* in *JMG Ultra Studio › Ask*, unless you say to change the sequence itself; project edits (labels, names, bins) get an **Undo that** button.

**Several chats at once:** press **+** for another chat while one is working. They think side by side and take turns in Premiere (one step at a time, so nothing collides). A dot on a chat’s tab shows it is working, waiting its turn, or waiting for you; a toast says when one in the background has finished. Chats are kept with the project (**Earlier…**).

**Which AI** (the second menu): Automatic (Settings’ choice), or pick Claude Opus / Sonnet / Haiku, Gemini or the local model for this chat – a quick cheap one for simple jobs, the best for a rough cut. **Stop** stops this chat only.

**What it spends** (the third menu, and the line under the chat box): choose how much one request may spend on AI before it stops to ask – 50¢, $2, $5, $10 – or **No spending limit**. At the limit it doesn’t give up: a **Spending check** card says what the request has used and you choose **Carry on** (as much again), **No limit for this chat**, or **Stop here** (what’s made so far is kept). After 25 steps it asks the same way. Under the chat box you always see what this chat has cost and the month’s total; each report says what that request cost and which AI did it. Your monthly AI limit in Settings always holds, whatever you choose here. Gemini’s free allowance and a local model cost nothing.

**What’s in the footage** (Settings › AI › Footage): Ultra Studio can look at your footage so Ask can find shots by what’s in them – *“the drone shots over water”*, *“put everything shot in the kitchen in a Kitchen bin”*, *“B-roll for Ruth’s line about the oven”* – and answer questions about a clip (*“is the sign readable in A001?”*).
- **Describe the footage** – type *describe my footage* in Ask, or tick it in **Overnight prep**. A few small stills of each clip (512 pixels wide: one for a short clip, up to four for a long one) are looked at, and a line or two is written: the kind of shot, where, who and what is in it, what happens, any writing in the picture, tags. The words are kept per file, so each clip is done once; after that, finding shots costs nothing.
- **Who looks is your choice.** *Claude or Gemini*: the best descriptions; the stills leave your computer, so it is **off until you switch it on for a project** (“small stills may be sent”), and it uses the cheapest model that does it well unless you pick another – Settings shows the cost per 100 clips. Or *a model on this computer*: nothing leaves; free; plainer descriptions.
- **Models on this computer are optional and never downloaded by default.** Settings lists each one with what it is for, where it is used, its size and the memory it wants, with **Download** and **Remove**. If a feature needs one that isn’t there, it asks first. They run in Ollama, a free app (ollama.com).
- **Looking at a clip for a question**: when the descriptions don’t answer it, Ask takes a few stills of the clips in question – after a **May I?** card when they leave your computer, naming the clips.
- Never video, never sound. Settings › What was sent to AI lists every still that went (which clip and time); the pictures themselves aren’t kept there. A project set to *nothing leaves this computer* only ever uses a model on this computer.
- Nothing pops up: stills are read inside the panel (or with ffmpeg if it’s installed). Clips the panel can’t read are listed and left for Overnight prep’s Selects step, which makes the small copies; Media Encoder is never started just for this.

**Without an AI key** the built-in commands still work: type one (or click an example) and it shows a plan to **Run**. With AI on, a short built-in command typed in a new chat also runs this way – instantly and free.

**Asking your interviews:** questions about what the interviews say (*where do the interviewees disagree?*, *a 45-second opening about why they came*, *who talks about their parents, and what do they say?*) are answered with a few sentences and the clips, in groups – each clip a whole stretch of speech, with a note on why it’s there and its length; the total shows against the length you asked for. Nothing changes in Premiere until you choose: play, move, take out, rename or recolour, then **New sequence** (each group in turn, in its colour, with a marker on every clip, group names as markers or a name layer, optionally in the order they were filmed), **Mark a sequence** (range markers on a string-out or your cut), **Themes** (one per group) or **Word or text**. Type a change under the answer – *shorter*, *only Ruth*, *split it before and after the move* – and it answers again in full. It reads what you chose to work on in Story, and the answers are kept with the project.

**Already in your cut**: ask *which stories about Grandma haven’t I used yet?* (or *what’s not in my cut*, *unused lines about faith*) with your cut open, and the answer leaves out every clip that’s already heard in that sequence. On any other answer, press **What’s already in my cut?**: each clip that’s in the open sequence is marked *in your cut* (or *partly*), and **Take those out** leaves only what you haven’t used. The AI is only told which transcript lines the cut has – never the sequence, its name or any footage.

In Premiere, Ask can colour clips in a bin (every use of them takes the colour) but not single clips on a timeline – Premiere gives add-ons no way to do that, so Ask says so and offers the bin colour or markers instead.

In DaVinci Resolve the edits are framing, opacity, colour labels, on/off, deleting and markers: Resolve doesn’t let add-ons add transitions, effects, speed changes, keyframes or text, and doesn’t say which timeline clips are selected (the clip under the playhead stands in).

**Recipes**: a chain of steps saved under a name – *Get interviews ready*, *Check and deliver*, or your own. After a plan or a request finishes, press **Save as a recipe**. Run one from the welcome's **Recipes** chips, **All recipes…**, or by typing *run my … recipe*. Steps run one after another on the open project; a step in words is asked in the chat, like typing it.
